title: On LLMs: Do the benefits outweigh the costs?
---

There's a rather common sentiment (on the internet anyway) that the utility of LLMs always usurps any negative criticisms. Built on stolen data? Oh well, the technology is here to stay. Energy use? Water? Cost of computing hardware? Bah, don't be such a luddite! This is _game changing_ stuff right here.

For an overview of the detractors, [see here](https://codeberg.org/ethical-foss/ai-free-foss-alternatives/src/branch/main/why_not_llms.md), with some more in the footnotes<sup id="fnref:1"><a href="#fn:1">1</a></sup>. In short, moral and ethical concerns unrelated to the effectiveness of the "tool." 

As for the benefits…
- faster coding? Maybe that’s a benefit. In my experience what to build was always the blocker over actually building a thing. 
- More code... Faster?  Are we counting LoC again?
- Faster PRs…

Actually why is faster a benefit? Why are we all so short of time!? Have we not already amassed capabilities unlike any other time in history? Is our progress not already outpacing the limits of physical reality? Of our earth?<sup id="fnref:2"><a href="#fn:2">2</a></sup>

**Are LLMs not just a continuation of an ideological view detached from the material world? AKA _Exponential economic growth._**

That’s the prevailing ideology we all live under, right? And it _is_ an ideology. It is not a material or psychological fact nor a Law of the Universe. It’s a social construct that has persisted for some hundreds of years, I'm sure evolving over hundreds prior.

Faster! Faster! Faster! More! More! More!

If only we reached some imagined threshold would all our problems vanish and we’d enter into [an age of bliss and utopia!](https://blog.samaltman.com/the-gentle-singularity) Thousands (millions, billions??) of lowly believers kneeling and praying for their sweet trickle-down to alleviate their pain and suffering… Keep enduring today for the prophecy of a better future will soon come! [Or will it?](https://www.cigionline.org/articles/the-abundance-paradox-artificial-general-intelligence-and-the-end-of-scarcity/)

**Do people not know they are living this ideology?**

We can either consciously recognize that our [collective behavior](https://www.cbc.ca/news/science/unep-emissions-overshoot-paris-agreement-9.7389086) is in [contradiction to a well-functioning planet](https://news.un.org/en/story/2026/10/1168677), or we can keep our heads in the fantasy-land in which we’ll somehow create something smarter than us (despite being built on our own words) that ushers in a new age where we’re no longer beholden to silly restrictions like the physical limits of the one known planet harbouring life... _any_ life at all mind you... 

The former sounds like rational behavior, at least to me. The latter sounds like the behavior of religious zealots: ignore actual, pressing issues of the day and put all faith into some outside entity and future salvation! 

[All hail the cult.](https://blog.samaltman.com/successful-people)

Note that if you question the prevailing ideology, you'll be labeled a commie, leftie, socialist, luddite, _whatever_, and derided as an enemy of all that is good and holy. Sorta like back when the church would call you a heretic when you [questioned](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornelius_Loos) [burning women alive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Witch_trials_in_the_early_modern_period). 

One of the biggest (and rightfully so) critiques of socialism/communism is the concentration of power. Yet, is techno-fascism and the absolute [units of money](https://www.theguardian.com/commentisfree/2025/sep/16/ai-wealth-inequality-cultural-division) going into LLMs not also leading to a [concentration of power](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0160791X24002677#abstracts)? I mean can any of us (at least as individuals and small groups) have even the slightest impact on the prevailing culture that a billionaire can?

Scientists have been warning us of our planetary degradations for decades… yet even that group can’t compete with the Cult of More. 

So, fuck it, make your little app. Generate those images. Automate your own clanker-hosted podcast! It’s neat! It’s cool. Whatever keeps us happy and compliant within the Religion of Infinite Growth!

It’s “[inevitable](https://www.businessinsider.com/tech-ceos-declare-agi-is-here-2026-10)” anyway. AGI, economic growth, progress. There’s no stopping what’s “[inevitable.](https://leonfurze.com/2025/04/28/the-myth-of-inevitable-ai/)” Or, what did zealots of yore used to say? It’s the [Will of God?](https://brianzahnd.com/2021/01/of-god-and-genocide/) Okie-doke. 

Well, at least we don't have <del>prophets</del> <ins>CEOs</ins> [predicting](https://gizmodo.com/the-doomsday-warnings-ai-ceos-gave-when-they-first-founded-their-companies-2000814278) the _End of the World_... Oh... I see.

How could we ever have seen this coming? Oh? They [told us](https://blog.samaltman.com/successful-people) they were out to create a religion. Well, o.k. then.

Are you sure you’re not living in the fantasy of religious extremists? Are you sure you’re not in a cult? 

...

Oh yeah, so, do the benefits outweight the costs? Look. I don't know what's best for you. But, I believe the following to both be true:

1. LLMs and the primary companies behind them are artifacts of a broken idealogy. Creating new things is wonderful. Creating things guided largely by a single belief that more profit is all that matters is troubling.
2. LLMs are, or can be, useful.

Evolving our culture to care more about people and—_hold on to your granola_—the planet seems more prudent than anything else. And, like, not just a select few scientists, activists, and tree huggers... but, you know, the majority of us. 

Humanity probably needs a culture shift if we want to continue to thrive.
